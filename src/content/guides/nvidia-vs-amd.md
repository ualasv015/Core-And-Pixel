There is no universal winner between NVIDIA and AMD. For a gaming PC, start with the games and features you use, then compare cards in the same price range. The brand name alone cannot tell you which card is faster or better value.

## How their GPU architectures differ

NVIDIA’s current GeForce RTX 50 Series uses the **Blackwell** architecture. RTX cards include dedicated RT cores for ray-tracing work and Tensor cores for AI features such as DLSS. NVIDIA also has a mature CUDA software ecosystem that matters in some rendering, video, machine-learning and scientific applications.

AMD’s Radeon RX 9000 Series uses **RDNA 4**. Its compute units include dedicated ray accelerators and AI accelerators, and the Radeon software stack includes FidelityFX Super Resolution (FSR). For example, the Radeon RX 9070 XT has 16 GB of GDDR6 memory and a 256-bit interface.

The two companies use different GPU designs and software, so core counts and clock speeds are not directly comparable. A card’s performance comes from the whole design: shader throughput, memory bandwidth, cache, power limits, drivers and how the game engine uses the GPU.

## What the benchmark evidence says

The GeForce RTX 5070 Ti and Radeon RX 9070 XT are close competitors. In TechSpot’s 52-game comparison, their overall results were effectively tied at 1440p, with the RTX card about 1.4% ahead; at native 4K it led by about 4%. The balance changes by title and preset. The RX 9070 XT often did well with ray tracing disabled, while the RTX 5070 Ti took clearer leads in some demanding ray-traced and path-traced games.

That is the useful distinction: NVIDIA has the stronger case for heavy ray tracing and path tracing, while Radeon remains highly competitive in ordinary rasterized gaming. The 5070 Ti also used less power in TechSpot’s comparison. Treat all these results as a snapshot of that review’s games, drivers and settings, not a ranking that applies to every game.

## Current price snapshot: US and Spain

The two cards launched at suggested prices of **$749 for the RTX 5070 Ti** and **$599 for the RX 9070 XT**. Actual listings are what matter when you buy, and late-2026 prices are well above those starting points.

The US listings may exclude sales tax; Spanish consumer prices include VAT. Check the exact offer before relying on this comparison.

At the sampled prices, I would **not pay about one-third more in Spain or nearly half more in the US for the RTX 5070 Ti solely for average raster FPS**. The 52-game test does not show a matching lead in conventional gaming performance. I would pay a premium for NVIDIA if ray tracing or path tracing is central to the games I play, if I need DLSS in supported titles, or if a specific creative application depends on CUDA. Otherwise, the RX 9070 XT is the value pick from these two listings.

## Which brand fits your needs?

- **Mostly 1440p gaming with ray tracing off:** lean Radeon when it is meaningfully cheaper. Compare the exact games and the card variant’s cooling and noise.
- **Ray tracing or path tracing is a priority:** lean GeForce, then check the game-specific benchmark. Some titles still favour Radeon, but NVIDIA’s advantage is more consistent in the heaviest effects.
- **Streaming, editing, 3D work or AI applications:** check the exact software. CUDA support can make NVIDIA the practical choice; other applications may perform well on either vendor.
- **Upscaling and generated frames:** compare image quality, latency and support in the games you actually play. A generated frame is not the same as a frame rendered from new input.
- **Buying on a strict budget:** price the complete system. A cheaper GPU that meets your monitor target can leave money for a better monitor, SSD or power supply.

## A simple buying checklist

1. List your games, resolution, quality settings and whether you will use ray tracing.
2. Compare native rendering results separately from upscaling and frame generation.
3. Check VRAM, power consumption, PSU connectors, card dimensions and noise reviews.
4. Compare the exact model prices in your own market, including delivery and tax.
5. Recheck the tests and prices on the day you buy.

**My pick at the prices above:** Radeon RX 9070 XT for value-focused gaming; GeForce RTX 5070 Ti only when its ray-tracing, DLSS or software advantages are worth the large premium to you. If prices fall closer to launch levels, run the same comparison again.
